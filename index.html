YOUR SCREEN. YOUR MAC.

Privacy Policy

Your screen stays on your Mac. This page explains how Screen Ruler reads pixels, what it saves, and how you stay in control.

Effective and last updated: October 18, 2026

At a glance

  • Screen images are processed locally and held in memory during a measurement session.
  • Saved sessions contain measurement values, labels, notes, display information, and local copies of images you explicitly export or copy.
  • Images are saved or copied only when you choose an image export action.
  • No account, advertising, third-party analytics SDKs, or developer-operated backend.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy applies to the Screen Ruler macOS app and its privacy policy and support website. Screen Ruler is maintained by PotatoPlanetStudios. You do not need a Screen Ruler account to use the app.

The app does not upload screen images, measurements, notes, or usage activity to the developer. We do not sell personal data, use advertising trackers, or include third-party analytics SDKs.

2. Screen processing stays on your Mac

With your macOS Screen Recording permission, Screen Ruler reads visible display pixels to measure boundaries, show a magnified view, and sample colors. A live session covers all connected displays by default; you can also choose a single display. Captured frames can include anything visible on those displays, including content from other apps.

Full-screen captures are held in memory. Freeze stops new capture and keeps the current images for measuring. Interact lets you use other apps; capture continues if you entered Interact from Live, and remains paused if you entered from Frozen. Choose Done or quit Screen Ruler to end capture and release those full-screen images. Only image crops you explicitly export or copy are retained with the session.

The app uses local display and application information to locate displays and exclude its own measuring windows from capture. It does not capture microphone or system audio, record a video, or upload screen content. The playground uses a generated image and needs no Screen Recording access.

3. Data stored on your Mac

Screen Ruler saves the information needed to keep your measurements and restore your preferred workspace. This can include:

  • Session names, dates, identifiers, and notes you enter.
  • Measurement types, coordinates, dimensions, labels, cursor intersections, and pinned state.
  • Display names and identifiers, logical dimensions, and captured image dimensions.
  • Interface language, units, detection tolerance, snapping, appearance, tool order, keyboard shortcuts, and panel positions.
  • The exact annotated PNG crops you export or copy, with their filenames, dimensions, timestamps, and whether they were saved or copied.

Session files are stored in a ScreenRuler folder within the app's local Application Support directory. Retained PNG crops are stored in its Images subfolder, grouped by session. Preferences are stored separately by macOS. Starting another session, opening another saved session, finishing, or quitting preserves the current measurements and image log locally. Screen images that you have not exported or copied are not retained.

Screen Ruler has no cloud synchronization service or automatic diagnostic upload. macOS may retain operating-system diagnostics under its own settings. Apple may make diagnostics available through its services according to your Apple privacy settings.

4. Exports and the clipboard

You can export measurement data as JSON and save an annotated screen crop as PNG. Image export shows a preview and lets you choose the display and measurements to include. Each image you successfully save or copy is also kept locally in the session's image log, where you can view, copy, or save it again after finishing. The resulting image can contain private information visible in the crop; review it before saving or sharing. Portable Session JSON contains measurements and notes only, not image files.

Copy actions place measurement text or an image on the macOS clipboard. Other apps and clipboard features you use, including Universal Clipboard or clipboard managers, may access, synchronize, or retain that content under their own settings.

Exported files remain where you save them until you delete them. A location managed by a cloud storage or backup service may synchronize your files. Screen Ruler does not control those services or automatically send exports to the developer.

5. macOS permissions

  • Screen Recording: allows live measurement of pixels from other apps. You can review or revoke access in System Settings → Privacy & Security. The category may be called Screen Recording or Screen & System Audio Recording depending on macOS; Screen Ruler uses only the screen portion.
  • Export destinations: macOS file pickers grant access to the locations you choose for exported session data and images.
  • Open at login: an optional setting registers Screen Ruler as a login item. Opening the app at login does not itself begin a screen measurement session.

The app does not request Accessibility, Input Monitoring, microphone, camera, or Full Disk Access. Its global shortcut and measuring controls process the input needed to operate the app locally.

6. Retention, deletion, and your choices

Saved measurements, session images, and preferences remain on your Mac until you change or delete them. Uninstalling the app alone may leave its local support files and preferences.

  • Stop capture: choose Done, quit the app, or revoke Screen Recording access. Closing the workspace window or choosing Interact does not necessarily end a live session.
  • Remove a measurement: use its trash button. Deletion is undoable during the current session; save the session to persist the updated collection.
  • Remove a saved session: use the trash button beside its name in Your sessions and confirm deletion. This removes its measurements, notes, and locally retained image crops. Delete all sessions removes the saved library after confirmation.
  • Clear the local session library: quit the app first, then remove its ScreenRuler Application Support folder. For the sandboxed app, this is normally ~/Library/Containers/com.PotatoPlanetStudios.ScreenRuler/Data/Library/Application Support/ScreenRuler/. Other builds may use ~/Library/Application Support/ScreenRuler/.
  • Preferences: macOS stores these under the app's com.PotatoPlanetStudios.ScreenRuler preferences domain, separately from session files. Older builds' custom preset data may remain in that domain but is no longer used. Contact support for help with a complete local reset.
  • Exports and backups: deleting a session removes its local image log but does not delete files exported to other locations, clipboard copies, or backups. Manage those separate copies in the apps and services where you keep them.

The developer cannot access or remotely delete information held only on your Mac.

7. This website and support requests

These static pages are hosted by GitHub Pages. We have added no analytics scripts, tracking cookies, external fonts, or contact forms. GitHub receives information needed to serve the pages, such as your IP address and request metadata, under the GitHub Privacy Statement. Opening a support or policy link from the app uses your browser.

If you email potatoplanet.studios@gmail.com, we receive your email address, message, and any attachments you choose to send. We use them to respond and troubleshoot. Correspondence may remain in the support mailbox until deleted; you can request deletion of messages we control. Our Gmail mailbox is provided by Google, whose processing is described in the Google Privacy Policy. Email services may retain copies under their own policies.

GitHub Issues are public and require a GitHub account. Your username and issue content are visible to others and remain on GitHub until removed, subject to GitHub's retention policies. You may edit or delete content where GitHub permits, or ask us for help removing content we control.

Do not post unredacted screenshots, private session notes, personal information, or private file paths in a public issue. Use email for private requests and share only what is needed to answer your question.
